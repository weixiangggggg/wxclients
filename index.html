01 / RETIREMENT PLANNING

Make room for your next chapter.

A starting estimate for the retirement lifestyle you have in mind.

Your retirement picture

Adjust planning assumptions

Guaranteed income is your estimate at retirement, such as CPF LIFE or a pension. This tool does not calculate CPF entitlements.

How this estimate works

Monthly spending today is increased by your inflation assumption until retirement. We subtract the income you enter to find the monthly gap. For simplicity, that gap then rises with inflation throughout retirement, so the model assumes your income rises with inflation too. Fixed income needs a more detailed review.

The target is the present value at retirement of end-of-month withdrawals, using an inflation-adjusted retirement return. Current savings and end-of-month contributions grow at the pre-retirement return. “Extra per month” is the additional constant contribution needed to close the estimated shortfall.

All returns are constant, effective annual assumptions after fees and taxes. The model excludes market fluctuations, sequence-of-returns risk, changing contributions, bequests and unexpected costs. Defaults are examples, not forecasts or recommendations.

02 / INVESTMENT PLANNING

See what consistency could build.

Explore a possible outcome. Change the assumptions to see a different picture.

Your investment scenario

The return is an illustrative annual assumption after fees and taxes, not a promised or recommended return.

How this estimate works

The effective annual return is converted to a monthly rate. The initial amount grows from the start; monthly contributions are added at the end of each month. Values are nominal and not adjusted for inflation. Actual investments can lose value, and returns will vary. This is not an illustration of any specific investment or insurance policy.

A few useful ideas

Reading your retirement estimate

Your retirement target is an estimate based on the spending, income and assumptions you entered. Try different retirement ages and spending amounts. A shortfall is a useful conversation starter, not a prediction of your future.

Understanding investment growth

Compounding means growth can build on earlier growth. Time and regular contributions affect the result, but market returns are uncertain. Try a lower or negative return assumption as well as your base scenario.

Making your budget practical

Start with recent statements, include irregular bills as monthly amounts, and avoid counting credit-card repayments twice if the purchases are already included. Keep savings allocations separate from spending.
