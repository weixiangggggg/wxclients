MY STORY

A promise that shaped my purpose

At 15, a kidney illness brought $180,000 in medical bills to my family.

I saw how much my parents carried for me. That experience opened my eyes to the power of financial planning and resilience. I made a promise: one day, I would stand on my own feet and make sure my parents would not have to worry again.

At 21, I became a licensed financial planner. Since then, I have built a portfolio of businesses, helped families secure over $10 million in coverage, and manage $1 million in assets.

How this estimate works

Part 1 calculates your target monthly retirement income as a percentage of your monthly spending today. If you turn on inflation, both spending and income targets increase by 3% annually until retirement; otherwise, no inflation is included. The savings target is the amount needed at retirement to cover the estimated monthly gap through your chosen planning age.

Part 2 grows your current savings and monthly contributions at the pre-retirement return, then compares that projection with the savings target. Any extra monthly amount is an estimate based on a constant return and contribution.

All returns are constant, effective annual assumptions after fees and taxes. The model excludes market fluctuations, sequence-of-returns risk, changing contributions, bequests and unexpected costs. Defaults are examples, not forecasts or recommendations.

Frequently asked questions

How much will I get from CPF LIFE?

It depends on your Retirement Account savings and the age you start. For members turning 55 in 2026, CPF Board estimates about S$950 a month from 65 with the Basic Retirement Sum, S$1,780 with the Full Retirement Sum and S$3,440 with the Enhanced Retirement Sum, on the Standard Plan.

What are the retirement sums in 2026?

For members turning 55 in 2026, the Basic Retirement Sum is S$110,200, the Full Retirement Sum is S$220,400 and the Enhanced Retirement Sum is S$440,800. The applicable BRS and FRS are fixed when you turn 55; the ERS is adjusted over time.

When do CPF LIFE payouts start?

You can choose a payout start age from 65 to 70. Deferring payouts can increase the monthly amount by up to 7% for each year deferred, or up to 35% if you defer from 65 to 70. The calculator uses CPF Board’s published figures at 65 and 70 and linearly interpolates between them.

How accurate is this estimate?

This is an illustration based on CPF Board’s published 2026 Standard Plan payout points for a male member, not a personalised quote. It uses straight-line interpolation between listed RA amounts and between ages 65 and 70. Your actual payout depends on your exact balance, birth year, sex, plan, interest and CPF Board’s calculations.

How do the Standard, Escalating and Basic Plans differ?

The Standard Plan gives level monthly payouts. The Escalating Plan starts lower and increases by 2% each year. The Basic Plan starts with lower payouts that may decrease over time. This estimator shows the Standard Plan only; use CPF Board’s estimator for the other plans.

Can I increase my CPF LIFE payout?

You may increase your Retirement Account savings by making eligible top-ups or CPF transfers, subject to applicable limits. You can also defer your payout start age, up to age 70, to receive a higher monthly payout.

What happens to my CPF LIFE savings when I die?

Any unused CPF LIFE premium balance, together with remaining CPF savings, is distributed to your beneficiaries. CPF LIFE provides monthly payouts for as long as you live.

Do I have to join CPF LIFE?

Eligible members born in 1958 or later with at least S$60,000 in retirement savings when payouts start are automatically included. Eligible members who are not automatically included may choose to join voluntarily. Check CPF Board for eligibility and the latest rules.

How we calculate this

  1. CPF Board publishes Standard Plan monthly payouts at six RA amounts for members turning 55 in 2026: S$50,000, S$110,200 (BRS), S$150,000, S$220,400 (FRS), S$300,000 and S$440,800 (ERS). Each has a payout from age 65 and age 70. Source: CPF Board payout estimates ↗
  2. For an RA amount between published points, we draw a straight line between the two payouts. Below S$50,000 we scale the S$50,000 payout down in proportion. Estimates stop at the 2026 ERS of S$440,800. Source: CPF Board retirement sums ↗
  3. For a start age between 65 and 70, we draw a straight line between the published age-65 and age-70 amounts. CPF describes deferred payouts as increasing by up to 7% for each year of deferral, up to 35% at age 70. Source: CPF LIFE and deferring payouts ↗
  4. Total paid out by a selected age is the estimated monthly payout multiplied by 12 and by the years between your payout start age and that age. CPF LIFE continues paying after the ages shown.
  5. Today’s-dollars values divide the nominal payout by 1 + your inflation assumption once for each year from the payout start age to the age in the table.
  6. If you have less than S$60,000 in retirement savings when payouts start, you are not automatically included in CPF LIFE, though eligible members can choose to join.

Rounding

CPF Board’s published payout figures are in whole dollars. The calculator interpolates without rounding and rounds displayed estimates to the nearest dollar. Your actual payout depends on your exact balance, interest and plan.

Worked example: the default case

  1. You turn 55 in 2026, set aside S$220,400 (the FRS) and start payouts at 70. Inflation is set at 2%.
  2. S$220,400 is a published point, so no interpolation is needed: CPF Board estimates S$2,380 a month from 70.
  3. A year of payouts is S$2,380 × 12 = S$28,560.
  4. Total paid by age 80: S$2,380 × 12 × 10 = S$285,600.
  5. In today’s dollars at age 80: S$2,380 ÷ 1.0210 ≈ S$1,952.
  6. Starting at 65 instead gives CPF Board’s published estimate of S$1,780 a month.

What this calculator does not cover

  • The Escalating and Basic Plans; CPF does not publish their payouts at fixed sums in this table.
  • Personalised CPF LIFE estimates using your exact balance, birth year and chosen plan.
  • Members turning 55 in other years, whose retirement sums and payouts differ.
  • Top-ups or withdrawals after 55; the estimate treats your RA amount at 55 as fixed.
  • Your beneficiaries’ exact bequest. Check the CPF Board estimator for a personalised calculation.

A few useful ideas

Reading your retirement estimate

Your retirement target is an estimate based on the spending, income and assumptions you entered. Try different retirement ages and spending amounts. A shortfall is a useful conversation starter, not a prediction of your future.

Understanding investment growth

Compounding means growth can build on earlier growth. Time and regular contributions affect the result, but market returns are uncertain. Try a lower or negative return assumption as well as your base scenario.

Making your budget practical

Start with recent statements, include irregular bills as monthly amounts, and avoid counting credit-card repayments twice if the purchases are already included. Keep savings allocations separate from spending.
