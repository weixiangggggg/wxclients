MY STORY

A promise that shaped my purpose

At 15, a kidney illness brought $180,000 in medical bills to my family.

I saw how much my parents carried for me. That experience opened my eyes to the power of financial planning and resilience. I made a promise: one day, I would stand on my own feet and make sure my parents would not have to worry again.

At 21, I became a licensed financial planner. Since then, I have built a portfolio of businesses, helped families secure over $10 million in coverage, and manage $1 million in assets.

Your CPF and savings

Payout estimates use age 65 as the start age. Enter savings outside CPF separately to avoid double-counting. The result projects your current OA and SA balances and salary to age 55, then models the RA set-aside using the 2026 Full Retirement Sum.

Calculate your savings target in Part 1 first, then enter your savings and CPF details here. Monthly contributions are estimated from your salary and CPF allocation rates.

How this estimate works

Part 1 calculates your target monthly retirement income as a percentage of your monthly spending today. If you turn on inflation, both spending and income targets increase by 3% annually until retirement; otherwise, no inflation is included. The savings target is the amount needed at retirement to cover the estimated monthly gap through your chosen planning age.

Part 2 grows your current savings and monthly contributions at the pre-retirement return, then compares that projection with the savings target. Any extra monthly amount is an estimate based on a constant return and contribution.

All returns are constant, effective annual assumptions after fees and taxes. The model excludes market fluctuations, sequence-of-returns risk, changing contributions, bequests and unexpected costs. Defaults are examples, not forecasts or recommendations.

How these estimates work

The effective annual return is converted to a monthly rate. In the scenario, the starting amount grows from the beginning and monthly contributions are added at each month end. Milestones show both elapsed years and your age.

The target calculator solves for a level month-end contribution using your current balance, annual net return and months remaining until your target age. Amounts are nominal and not adjusted for inflation. Actual investments can lose value, and returns will vary. These estimates are not forecasts or recommendations.

A few useful ideas

Reading your retirement estimate

Your retirement target is an estimate based on the spending, income and assumptions you entered. Try different retirement ages and spending amounts. A shortfall is a useful conversation starter, not a prediction of your future.

Understanding investment growth

Compounding means growth can build on earlier growth. Time and regular contributions affect the result, but market returns are uncertain. Try a lower or negative return assumption as well as your base scenario.

Making your budget practical

Start with recent statements, include irregular bills as monthly amounts, and avoid counting credit-card repayments twice if the purchases are already included. Keep savings allocations separate from spending.
